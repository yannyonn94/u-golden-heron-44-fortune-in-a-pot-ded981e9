# Concept Test — Fortune in a Pot

Scoring rubric (max 25): fortune cadence (5) + specific-to-this-window (5)
+ funny, small joke that lands (5) + kind/warm (5) + clean read-aloud,
one line, no padding (5).

## V1 — 23/25 — WINNER

> Soon, a sunbeam will find your favorite leaf at 4 p.m., and the cat will admire you from a respectful distance.

- 111 characters, 111 bytes, pure ASCII.
- Cadence 5/5: "Soon, ... will ..." is a classic fortune opening; two-part
  prediction with a gentle turn.
- Specific-to-this-window 5/5: "your favorite leaf", "4 p.m." (the afternoon
  sun on a windowsill), "the cat" — this could only be said about this plant
  in this window, not any plant in any room.
- Funny 4/5: the cat admiring "from a respectful distance" is a small,
  visual joke plant lovers recognise instantly; it lands without a pun.
- Kind 5/5: warm, no doom, no withering, no mockery — the cat is kept at a
  safe, admiring distance.
- Clean read 4/5: reads smoothly aloud; "at 4 p.m." is the only slight
  stumble, and it earns its place by anchoring the scene.

## V2 — 22/25

> By Thursday you will outgrow this pot, and your keeper will admit you were right about the sunny spot all along.

- 112 characters, 112 bytes, pure ASCII.
- Cadence 5/5: "By Thursday you will ..." is strong fortune rhythm.
- Specific-to-this-window 4/5: "this pot" and "the sunny spot" ground it in
  the window, but the scene is less vivid than V1's sunbeam and cat.
- Funny 4/5: the plant being "right all along" is charming and lands well.
- Kind 5/5: warm, playful, no doom.
- Clean read 4/5: smooth, slightly longer breath than V1.

## V3 — 19/25

> A tall stranger will water you twice this week, and the third time, it will be love — in a brand-new terracotta pot.

- 116 characters, 118 bytes.
- Cadence 4/5: fortune-shaped, but the em dash and the third clause make it
  feel like a joke with a setup rather than a prediction.
- Specific-to-this-window 3/5: "terracotta pot" is concrete, but "a tall
  stranger" could belong to any fortune, plant or not.
- Funny 4/5: "the third time, it will be love" is sweet and funny.
- Kind 4/5: warm, though the romance framing is about the keeper's feelings
  as much as the plant's future.
- Clean read 4/5: the em dash reads fine aloud, but the line is the longest
  of the three and the least tight.

## Judge's-view self-check (plain text, ~2 seconds)

Read aloud as plain text, V1 lands immediately: the sunbeam finding "your
favorite leaf at 4 p.m." paints the windowsill in one breath, and "the cat
will admire you from a respectful distance" gets the laugh — warm, specific,
and unmistakably a fortune for this plant. No padding, no stumble.

## Why V1 wins

V1 scores highest on the two criteria the judge weighs first after
"fortune for a houseplant": it is the most specific to *this* plant in
*this* window (sunbeam at 4 p.m., favorite leaf, the cat) and its joke is
the smallest and most plant-lover-shaped of the three — a cat admiring from
a respectful distance. It is also the shortest (111 bytes), which wins the
size tie-break if rates ever tie. No fourth variant was proposed: none of
the alternatives tried beat V1's combination of window-specificity and a
joke that lands in one breath.

## Winner (implemented in main.py)

> Soon, a sunbeam will find your favorite leaf at 4 p.m., and the cat will admire you from a respectful distance.
